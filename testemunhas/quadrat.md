# Quadrato

Quadrato entrega uma apologia ao imperador Adriano, por volta de 125. O livro se perdeu. Eusébio, *História eclesiástica* 4,3, conserva um parágrafo. É o texto inteiro que resta. Quadrato diz que as pessoas curadas e ressuscitadas por Jesus ainda viviam no seu tempo, e que o Salvador foi visto depois de morto.

Texto inglês de Alexander Roberts e James Donaldson, *Ante-Nicene Fathers* (1885–1887), domínio público. Não é a revisão posterior do site New Advent.

Fragments of Quadratus of Athens

Quadratus, Bishop of
Athens.

------------

[a.d. 126.] Quadratus
is spoken of by Eusebius as a "man of understanding and of Apostolic faith." And
he celebrates Aristides as a man of similar character. These were the earliest
apologists; both addressed their writings to Hadrian, and they were extant and
valued in the churches in the time of Eusebius.

From the Apology for the Christian
 Religion.

Our Saviour's works, moreover, were always present: for they were real,
consisting of those who had been healed of their diseases, those who had
been raised from the dead; who were not only seen whilst they were being healed
and raised up, but were afterwards constantly present. Nor did they
remain only during the sojourn of the Saviour on earth, but also a
considerable time after His departure; and, indeed, some of them have survived
even down to our own times.
